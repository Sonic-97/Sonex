## 1. Resolved: 4 Conflicting Policies

### Conflict 1: Order Cancellation Role Rule

**Conflict:** ORD-004 says "any authenticated user can cancel." ORD-002 transition table shows "Any | CANCELLED | Any authenticated user." But ORD-002 Future Notes say "role check needed."

**Resolution:**
- **Cancellation SHALL require role authorization.** The current "any authenticated user" is a security gap.
- **Rule:** OWNER can cancel ANY order in any state. BARISTA can cancel in-cafe orders only (NEW, PREPARING, READY, DELIVERED — not COMPLETED). DRIVER can cancel their own assigned delivery orders (NEW, CONFIRMED, PREPARING, READY — not after PICKED_UP).
- **Paid-order rule:** Any order that has received payment (status = PAID or DELIVERED+paid) requires OWNER authorization to cancel, regardless of who created it. This prevents casual voiding of financial transactions.
- **Implementation:** The `cancel()` function MUST route through the ORD-002 state machine or a dedicated cancellation gate that enforces the role check. No bypass path allowed.
- **Transition table entry updated to:**

| From | To | Allowed Roles | Side Effects |
|------|----|---------------|-------------|
| Any non-terminal | CANCELLED | OWNER (all states); BARISTA (in-cafe only); DRIVER (own orders, unpaid only) | cancelledAt, inventory restored, financial reversal if paid |

**Impacts:** Update ORD-002 transition table. Update ORD-004 Security Constraints. Update EMP-003 role definitions.

### Conflict 2: Recipe Cost Waste Multiplier

**Conflict:** REC-002 rule 1 includes waste multiplier (`quantity × (1 + waste%) × costPerUnit`). REC-003 rule 1 (Ingredient Cost) uses straight `quantity × costPerUnit` with no waste.

**Resolution:**
- **REC-003 Ingredient Cost SHALL include waste multiplier**, matching REC-002's formula exactly: `quantity × (1 + wastePercent / 100) × costPerUnit`.
- **Rationale:** Waste is a real production cost. The ingredient you purchase is more than the ingredient that goes into the product. The 5-component breakdown should reflect true total cost. Consistency between REC-002 and REC-003 is more important than maintaining separate formulas.
- REC-003 rule 1 is amended to: *"Ingredient Cost: sum of (quantity × (1 + wastePercent / 100) × costPerUnit) for all recipe ingredients."*
- The existing costing scenarios (costing-009 through costing-013) will need their expected values recalculated in Phase P1 to reflect the corrected formula.

**Impacts:** Update REC-003 Business Rules rule 1. Recompute costing scenarios 009-013 in P1.

### Conflict 3: In-Cafe Payment Status Vocabulary

**Conflict:** In-cafe orders use NOT_PAID/PARTIALLY_PAID/PAID/VOID. Delivery orders use UNPAID/PARTIAL_PAYMENT/PAID. Same concept, different enums.

**Resolution:**
- **Standardize to a single payment status set across ALL order types:** `UNPAID` | `PARTIALLY_PAID` | `PAID`.
- **Rationale for choices:**
  - `UNPAID` over `NOT_PAID` — more natural English, matches existing delivery set.
  - `PARTIALLY_PAID` over `PARTIAL_PAYMENT` — clearer as a status (describes the order, not the action).
  - `PAID` is already consistent.
- **VOID status removed from payment vocabulary.** VOID is an ORDER status (see ORD-003), not a payment status. Voided orders may have any payment status (they were paid before voiding).
- Policy PAY-001 updated to use `UNPAID`, `PARTIALLY_PAID`, `PAID`.

**Impacts:** Update PAY-001 rule 8. Update ORD-003 (VOID is order status, not payment status). Update all code references.

### Conflict 4: Refrigerator Stock Sync Direction

**Conflict:** Product refrigeratorStock → inventory.currentQty (one-way). Manual inventory adjustments do NOT reverse-sync to product.

**Resolution:**
- **Keep one-way sync: product → inventory. Manual inventory adjustments MUST NOT reverse-sync.**
- **Rationale:** The product's `refrigeratorStock` is the operational stock count used at POS (how many units available to sell). Direct inventory adjustments are accounting/cost corrections that should not silently override the operational count.
- **Safety mechanism:** When a manual inventory adjustment targets an inventory record linked to a refrigerated product, the system SHALL:
  1. Accept the adjustment (inventory is the source of truth for costing)
  2. Emit a warning event/notification: `REFRIGERATED_STOCK_DESYNC`
  3. NOT modify the product's `refrigeratorStock`
- **Operational process:** Staff SHOULD adjust refrigerated stock through the product form, not through direct inventory adjustment.
- INV-006 rule 4 updated to explicitly document the warning mechanism and direct staff to use the product form.

**Impacts:** Update INV-006 Business Rules. Add warning notification type to NOT-001.

### Ambiguity 2: Driver State Machine Bypass

**Issue:** `assignToOrder` and `completeDelivery` bypass the ORD-002 state machine. Orders can jump from NEW to PICKED_UP or CONFIRMED to DELIVERED without intermediate states.

**Resolution:**
- **All driver actions MUST go through the ORD-002 state machine.** DRV-001 is the business policy for driver workflow; ORD-002 is the implementation mechanism.
- The driver's actions correspond to ORD-002 transitions:
  - Accept/Assign → sets `assignedDriverId`, order remains at current status
  - Pick up → READY → PICKED_UP (requires current = READY)
  - Deliver → PICKED_UP → DELIVERED (requires current = PICKED_UP)
  - Collect payment → DELIVERED → PAID (requires current = DELIVERED)
- The driver controller methods MUST validate current status before transitioning. No direct status-setting allowed.
- DRV-001 rules 3-5 updated to reference the ORD-002 transition table explicitly.

**Impacts:** Update DRV-001 Business Rules to reference ORD-002 transitions. Driver controllers must validate status.

### Ambiguity 4: PREPARING Status

**Issue:** PREPARING exists in TS enum and state machine but absent from some schema definitions. Inconsistent documentation (8 vs 9 states).

**Resolution:**
- **PREPARING is a VALID status in ALL representations.** The canonical state set is 9 states: NEW, CONFIRMED, PREPARING, READY, PICKED_UP, DELIVERED, PAID, CLOSED, CANCELLED.
- **UX shortcut permitted:** A "Mark Ready" button MAY advance directly from CONFIRMED to READY. When this shortcut is used, the system SHALL auto-set the PREPARING timestamp to the same time as the READY timestamp. This is a UI convenience, not a state machine change.
- ORD-002 transition table updated to show both paths: CONFIRMED→PREPARING→READY (standard) and CONFIRMED→READY (shortcut, with PREPARING auto-set).
- ORD-003 (In-Cafe) already includes PREPARING, which is correct.

**Impacts:** Update ORD-002 to document the PREPARING shortcut. Ensure all schema definitions include PREPARING.

### Ambiguity 5: In-Cafe vs Delivery Status Machines

**Issue:** Two different machines for different order types. Semantics differ.

**Resolution:**
- **Both machines are intentionally different and both are valid.** In-cafe orders have a simpler lifecycle because they don't require confirmation, driver assignment, or pickup.
- **Explicit mapping documented in both ORD-002 and ORD-003:**

| Concept | Delivery Machine | In-Cafe Machine |
|---------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Order placed | NEW | NEW |
| Acknowledged | CONFIRMED | *(skipped)* |
| Being made | PREPARING | PREPARING |
| Ready to serve | READY | READY |
| Left premises/counter | PICKED_UP | *(skipped)* |
| Given to customer | DELIVERED | DELIVERED |
| Payment handled | PAID | *(in-cafe payment is at time of service)* |
| Terminal success | CLOSED | COMPLETED |
| Terminal cancel | CANCELLED | VOID |

- **COMPLETED ≡ CLOSED** (both terminal success states, both prevent further modifications).
- **VOID ≡ CANCELLED** (both terminal cancellation states, both trigger inventory restoration).
- The i-cafe machine uses DELIVERED → COMPLETED (delivered to table → shift close). The delivery machine uses DELIVERED → PAID → CLOSED (delivered → payment → shift close).

**Impacts:** Add explicit mapping tables to ORD-002 and ORD-003.

### Ambiguity 7: employeeId vs staffId (createdById vs employeeId)

**Issue:** Orders have both `createdById` (staff who processed the order) and `employeeId` (staff "brought" the order / attributed for KPI). Semantics may confuse.

**Resolution:**
- **Rename fields in all policies for clarity:**
  - `createdBy` (aka `staffId` / `createdById`): The staff member who ACTED on the order (created, processed payment, etc.)
  - `attributedTo` (aka `employeeId`): The staff member who BROUGHT the order (for KPI/reporting). This is a business attribution, not necessarily the actor.
- **Policy clarification:**
  - `createdBy` is set automatically based on the authenticated user who performs the action.
  - `attributedTo` is optionally set by the barista at POS (e.g., "this order was brought by Ali").
  - Both fields coexist on the Order record.
- **KPI scoring (EMP-006)** uses `attributedTo` for "orders brought by" and `createdBy` for "orders processed by."
  - KPI Score = (paidOrders where attributedTo matches / totalOrders where attributedTo matches) × 100.
  - Optional: separate KPI for "orders processed" (createdBy).

**Impacts:** Update ORD-001, EMP-006, and all policies referencing these fields. Use `createdBy` and `attributedTo` instead of `staffId` and `employeeId`.

### Ambiguity 8: Expense Split 50/50

**Issue:** REC-003 splits operational expenses 50/50 into Operational and Miscellaneous. Rationale undocumented.

**Resolution:**
- **50/50 split is APPROVED as the default allocation.** Rationale documented: "The 50/50 split is a practical default that separates directly attributable operational costs (rent, salaries, utilities) from general overhead and hard-to-categorize costs (maintenance, supplies, miscellaneous). Pending a more granular cost allocation engine (e.g., activity-based costing per department), the 50/50 default provides a reasonable first approximation."
- REC-003 rule 7 updated to include this rationale.
- Future Extension Note added: "Cost allocation methodology may be refined with activity-based costing in a future phase."

**Impacts:** Add rationale to REC-003 rule 7. Add Future Extension Note.

1. **ORD-003** (In-Cafe Order Flow)
2. **PAY-002** (Driver Payment Collection)
3. **INV-003** (Inventory Reservation) — **CRITICAL** — offline overselling risk
4. **DRV-001** (Driver Delivery) — combined with PAY-002

**Recommended offline behaviours for these gaps (to be added to Constitution v1.0):**

- **ORD-003 (In-Cafe Offline):** In-cafe orders are created locally with pending sync. Status transitions (NEW→PREPARING→READY→DELIVERED→COMPLETED) are allowed locally. VOID requires online authorization if the order was paid (due to cash wallet adjustment). Unpaid voids can proceed locally.

- **INV-003 (Reservation Offline):** Inventory reservation during offline POS is deferred. The order is created without reservation. On sync, if stock is insufficient, the order is flagged for manual review (not rejected automatically, since the customer has already been served). This is a business risk decision: serve first, reconcile later.

- **PAY-002 / DRV-001 (Driver Offline):** Driver actions are not supported offline. Drivers operate with real-time assignment and tracking. If connectivity is lost mid-delivery, the driver can complete the delivery using cached order data, but payment collection is deferred until online (card authorization required) or handled as cash-on-delivery with offline queue.

## 7. Remaining Open Questions

1. **Cash float management:** No policy exists for beginning/end-of-shift cash float. This is classified as Required (P3) but should be noted as a gap in the daily closing workflow.

2. **Desktop scenario runner scope:** With the P0.6 resolutions, 3 of the 4 conflicting policies affect code paths that exist on Desktop (order cancellation UI, payment status mapping, stock sync direction). The Desktop scenario runner must be updated in P1 to verify the resolved behaviours.

3. **INV-003 offline risk acceptance:** The decision to defer inventory reservation during offline orders (serve first, reconcile later) is a BUSINESS RISK decision. Sign-off from Operations Director is recommended before P1.

4. **Paid-order cancellation rule:** The resolution states OWNER-only for paid-order cancellation, but this creates a potential bottleneck if the owner is unavailable. Consider: should BARISTA be able to cancel paid orders under a certain threshold (e.g., < 100 EGP)? This is an open design question for P1.
